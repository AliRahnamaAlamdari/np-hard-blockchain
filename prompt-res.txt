4. Prioritized improvements — reviewer-facing

P0 — I'd expect a reject-or-major-revision without these.

1. Name and justify the VRF; the current primitive likely breaks G3 and G4. ECDSA is not a VRF — its per-signature nonce is free, so one PoW seed yields many valid task assignments. Measured: 84.7 distinct tasks (of 256) from a single seed in 100 evaluations, every proof passing validation. Grinding cost collapses from 4,096 hashes/task to 48 — an 85× reduction, directly falsifying the k/p bound in §Resistance to Task Grinding. With RFC 9381 ECVRF it is pinned at exactly 1. Cost: low — swap the primitive, cite RFC 9381, rerun. Highest value-per-hour in this list.
2. The 155,000× asymmetry is not reproducible, and the arithmetic suggests a unit error. Your reported 0.001958 "ms" for VRF verification matches my measured ECDSA verify of 1.980 ms to within 1% — i.e. the column looks like seconds mislabelled as ms. Under that reading the true ratio is 785.08 / 5.069 = 155×, not 155,000×. Under the alternative reading (genuinely ms), no elliptic-curve verification is that fast and the "VRF" must be a keyless hash. Either way the headline is wrong by ~1000×. Also: the ratio is not a protocol constant — it scales with N. Cost: low, but it touches the abstract.
3. The 90.6% claim excludes all framework overhead. Your two columns sum to 100% by construction because only PoW+solve enter the denominator. Adding the framework's own costs: 90.86% → 60.02% (miner-side VRF/Merkle/packaging) → 26.02% (network-wide, incl. validation at 5 nodes). At the Low tier the crypto costs more than the useful work. Cost: low — one extra column. Reframes the contribution honestly rather than weakening it.
4. Reconcile the three solve times (I7) and fix Algorithm 1's nonce (I2). Cost: trivial. Both are things a careful reviewer will catch.

P1 — likely explicit reviewer requests.

5. Error bars and multi-seed runs. You run 50 repetitions for Table 4 and then report only means. No result has a std or CI. Cost: low — you already have the data.
6. Statistical testing. Fairness is claimed from one realization with no test. χ² on your own counts gives χ²=2.92, p=0.571 — supports your claim, and costs one line. Cost: trivial.
7. Component ablations. Table 1 argues each component's necessity rhetorically. I turned each row into a measurement; three of four attacks are cleanly blocked by the responsible check. Note the honest nuance: the "miner-chosen easy tasks" attack only bites when the repository has heterogeneous difficulty — which your "difficulty tiers" component would create but which is never implemented (U8). Cost: medium; my ablation_components is a starting point.
8. Complexity/cost analysis. (N-1)! is a property of your solver, not the problem. Held-Karp returns the identical optimum 64× faster at N=10 (≈4,000× extrapolated to N=12). A miner with better solver engineering gets a large, fully-valid mining edge — a real centralization concern that undercuts the fairness claim. Cost: medium. This is the most interesting new result in my sweep.

P2 — strengthens the paper.

9. Heterogeneous-power fairness. Equal-power testing makes Jain≈1 a foregone conclusion. Measured: Jain falls 0.977 → 0.458 as power concentrates, while block share stays correctly proportional (p ≥ 0.21). The protocol looks good here — but only a heterogeneous test can show it. Cost: low.
10. Repository size N. Never stated, despite i = Integer(y) mod N being the core rule. Selection stays uniform to N=1024 with only logarithmic proof growth (132→330 B) — so your own anti-precomputation defence is cheap. Also worth a sentence on modulo bias if the VRF output is ever truncated. Cost: low.
11. A quantitative baseline. Table 7 is qualitative with High/Medium/Low ratings and no rubric. Even a hash-only PoW control would let you say something measured. Cost: high.
12. TSPLIB instances. Random uniform points are the easiest possible TSP family. Cost: medium.
13. Failure-case analysis (what happens when a solve overruns, liveness under difficulty variance — G6 is stated but never tested) and reproducibility artifacts (the cited GitHub repo, seeds, exact configs). Cost: low–medium.
